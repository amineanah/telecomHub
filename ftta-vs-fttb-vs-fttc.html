💡 FTTA vs FTTB vs FTTC

Understanding the Main Fiber Access Architectures

FTTA, FTTB, and FTTC are common fiber access deployment models used by operators to deliver broadband services closer to the end user. The key difference lies in how far the fiber extends and how the final connection to the customer is completed.

🏢

FTTB

Fiber to the Building means the fiber reaches the building boundary, and the last connection is completed using copper or internal distribution.

  • Fiber terminates at the building
  • Shared among multiple users inside the building
  • Common in apartments and offices
  • Lower cost than FTTH
  • Good balance of performance and economics
🏠

FTTC

Fiber to the Curb brings the fiber close to the customer, usually near the street or curb, then copper continues for the final segment.

  • Fiber reaches the curb or cabinet
  • Last mile uses copper DSL/VDSL
  • Cost-effective upgrade path
  • Shorter copper segment than legacy networks
  • Popular in urban and suburban areas
💡

FTTA

Fiber to the Antenna is used mainly in mobile network infrastructure, where the fiber connects the base station equipment to the antenna site.

  • Fiber runs to the antenna site
  • Supports 4G/5G radio equipment
  • Used in distributed / remote radio unit deployments
  • Reduces RF loss over long coax runs
  • Essential for modern mobile networks

Architecture Fiber Endpoint Final Connection Best Use Case
FTTC Curb / cabinet Copper (DSL/VDSL) Cost-efficient broadband upgrades
FTTB Building Internal distribution / copper Apartment blocks, offices, multi-dwelling buildings

🏢 FTTB: Fiber to the Building

FTTB is often used in residential and commercial buildings where the fiber reaches the building entrance or basement and then distributes the service inside the property. This model is attractive when a building already has internal distribution infrastructure and when operators want to serve multiple users efficiently.

It offers a better balance between cost and performance than fully extending fiber to each unit. In many multi-dwelling buildings, it is one of the most practical fiber access strategies.

Best for: apartment buildings, office campuses, hotels, and buildings with shared internal networks.

🚧 FTTC: Fiber to the Curb

FTTC moves the fiber closer to the subscriber but stops before the last part of the connection. The final segment to the home or business uses copper-based technology, such as VDSL, which provides a faster and more stable connection than legacy DSL while remaining cost effective.

This approach is typically used where deploying full fiber all the way to the end user is not yet economically justified. It is an intermediate step for operators upgrading a network without massive civil work.

Best for: urban and suburban residential upgrades where copper last-mile economics still matter.

💡 FTTA: Fiber to the Antenna

FTTA is not primarily a residential architecture. It is a mobile network solution where fiber connects the baseband unit or centralized equipment to a remotely located antenna or radio unit. This reduces signal loss compared to long coaxial runs and supports the higher density and bandwidth needs of 4G and 5G.

Because the RF signal is sensitive to losses, fiber becomes the preferred transport medium between the radio equipment and the antenna site, especially in dense urban deployments.

Best for: 4G/5G radio access networks, urban densification, and sites with strict performance demands.

✓ Which One is Better?

There is no single “best” architecture. The correct choice depends on the service type, network strategy, and cost constraints:

FTTC is often a fast and affordable upgrade path.

FTTB is a stronger choice when serving multi-dwelling buildings efficiently.

FTTA is essential for mobile networks where the fiber must feed radio equipment at the network edge.

In short, FTTC is cost-focused, FTTB is building-focused, and FTTA is mobile-network-focused.
